COMMERCIAL BACKGROUND & CHALLENGE:
Imported Tanzanian toor dal provides an attractive raw material procurement advantage of approximately ₹18–22/kg compared to domestic Indian varieties. However, commercial uptake is severely restricted by extended cooking latency: requiring ~6 pressure-cooker whistles (~45–55 minutes) under domestic conditions compared to ~3 whistles (~20–25 minutes) for standard Indian pulses, often leaving persistent hard grains and non-uniform texture.

PHASE 1 DIAGNOSTIC SCOPE & DELIVERABLES:
Our Phase 1 program replaces qualitative kitchen observations with a rigorous 11-module laboratory investigation to pinpoint the underlying biophysical and chemical causes:
1. Physical Characterization & Instrumental Hardness Profile (1000-grain weight, bulk density, digital micrometry, texture analyzer breaking force).
2. Proximate Composition (moisture, crude protein, crude fat, crude fiber, ash, starch via AOAC/FSSAI methods).
3. Starch & Thermal Gelatinization (DSC onset, peak, conclusion temperatures, transition enthalpy, amylose:amylopectin ratio).
4. Hydration Kinetics & Water Absorption Index (multi-temperature soaking curves at 25°C, 45°C, 65°C).
5. Cell-Wall & Pectin Characterization (fractionation of water-soluble, chelator-soluble EDTA, and alkali-soluble pectins; degree of esterification).
6. Mineral Profile & Phytate Interactions (ICP-OES Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, Fe, P, and phytate:divalent cation molar ratios).
7. Standardized Objective Cooking Performance (instrumented pressure vessel trials with cooked core percentage < 2.0% objective endpoint).
8. Integrated Root-Cause Synthesis & Prioritized Phase 2 Intervention Recommendations.
