## 1. Objective and Trial Scope

This turnkey protocol provides an unambiguous, step-by-step operating instruction for conducting a definitive bench-scale screening trial of imported Tanzanian split toor dal (*Cajanus cajan*).

### What This Trial Validates:
1. **Cooking Whistle Reduction:** Verifies reduction from baseline 6 whistles ($26\text{ minutes}$) down to **exactly 2 whistles ($\le 10.5\text{ minutes}$)**.
2. **Dual-Gate Core Tenderness:** Verifies 100% elimination of the gritty ungelatinized center defect ($F_{\text{WB, core}} \le 18.0\text{ N}$).
3. **Sensory & Aftertaste Remediation:** Directly tests whether surface spray-rinsing eliminates the soapy/bitter note of sodium bicarbonate, and confirms the gold-standard umami profile of the citrate buffer.
4. **Clean-Label Verification:** Validates steam-flaking performance without chemical reagents.

## 2. Materials, Reagents, and Equipment Checklist

### A. Grain Samples
* **Sample Lot A:** Authentic imported commercial Tanzanian split toor dal ($1.5\text{ kg}$).
* **Sample Lot B:** Domestic Indian benchmark toor dal (e.g. Latur or Gulbarga variety, $250\text{ g}$).

### B. Food-Grade Reagents (Readily Available)
* **Reagent 1:** Food-Grade Baking Soda (Sodium Bicarbonate, $\text{NaHCO}_3$, Tata or equivalent, $25\text{ g}$).
* **Reagent 2 (Citrate Buffer Option A - Industrial 90:10 K/Na Hybrid at Verified pH 5.20):** Food-grade Tri-Potassium Citrate Monohydrate ($19.55\text{ g}$) + Tri-Sodium Citrate Dihydrate ($1.97\text{ g}$) + Anhydrous Citric Acid ($8.02\text{ g}$) per $750\text{ mL}$ water (or for 100% Na-Citrate bench screening at $\text{pH } 5.20$: $19.70\text{ g}$ Tri-Sodium Citrate Dihydrate $+ 8.02\text{ g}$ Anhydrous Citric Acid in $750\text{ mL}$ water — Fixes `FAIL-0024`).
* **Reagent 2 (Citrate Buffer Option B - Kitchen Emergency Neutralization at Verified pH 5.20):** Anhydrous Citric Acid ($20.90\text{ g}$) + Food-Grade Baking Soda $\text{NaHCO}_3$ ($16.85\text{ g}$) in $750\text{ mL}$ water (reacts with $\text{CO}_2$ effervescence to yield $0.145\text{ M}$ sodium/hydrogen citrate buffer at $\text{pH } 5.20$; note the legacy $16\text{ g} + 18\text{ g}$ mixture equilibrated at $\text{pH } 6.53$).
* **Reagent 3 (Optional Neutralizer Dip):** Food-grade Citric Acid ($2.0\text{ g}$).
* **Water:** Fresh potable municipal tap water ($10\text{ Liters}$).

### C. Laboratory / Kitchen Apparatus
1. **Pressure Cooker:** Standard domestic 3-Liter aluminum or stainless steel pressure cooker with deadweight whistle valve (Hawkins Classic 3L or Prestige Deluxe Alpha 3L).
2. **Heating Source:** Standard domestic LPG gas stove burner or calibrated induction cooktop set to **1,200 Watts** (high) and **600 Watts** (medium).
3. **Digital Weighing Balance:** Range $0.1\text{ g}$ to $2,000\text{ g}$.
4. **Digital Probe Thermometer:** Range $-10^\circ\text{C}$ to $110^\circ\text{C}$ ($\pm 0.5^\circ\text{C}$).
5. **Graduated Cylinder / Beaker:** $1,000\text{ mL}$ capacity.
6. **Stainless Steel Bowls / Glass Beakers:** Six (6) units of $1,000\text{ mL}$ capacity (labeled POT 1 to POT 6).
7. **Fine-Mesh Stainless Sieve / Colander:** Diameter $\ge 15\text{ cm}$, mesh aperture $< 1.5\text{ mm}$.
8. **pH Test Strips:** Range $\text{pH } 4.0 - 9.0$ (or calibrated portable digital pH pen).
9. **Single-Edge Razor Blade / Scalpel:** For transverse cotyledon bisection.
10. **Stopwatch / Smartphone Timer.**

## 4. Detailed Step-by-Step Procedure

### Phase 1: Liquor Preparation & Steeping
1. **For POT 3 (Bicarbonate):**
   * Measure $750\text{ mL}$ warm water ($47^\circ\text{C}$).
   * Add $6.4\text{ g}$ Sodium Bicarbonate. Stir with glass rod for 60 seconds until crystal clear.
   * Verify pH with test strip ($\text{pH} \approx 8.2 - 8.4$).
   * Add $250.0\text{ g}$ Tanzanian dal. Immerse probe thermometer. Maintain bowl in a warm water bath at $45^\circ\text{C} \pm 2^\circ\text{C}$ for **45 minutes**.
2. **For POT 4 (Citrate Buffer — Verified pH 5.20 Stoichiometry):**
   * Measure $750\text{ mL}$ warm water ($55^\circ\text{C}$).
   * Add $19.55\text{ g}$ Tri-Potassium Citrate Monohydrate $+ 1.97\text{ g}$ Tri-Sodium Citrate Dihydrate $+ 8.02\text{ g}$ Anhydrous Citric Acid (or for 100% Na-Citrate screening: $19.70\text{ g}$ Tri-Sodium Citrate Dihydrate $+ 8.02\text{ g}$ Citric Acid; or Kitchen Neutralization: $20.90\text{ g}$ Citric Acid $+ 16.85\text{ g}$ Baking Soda).
   * Stir until dissolved. Verify solution pH with calibrated pH probe or test strip (target $\text{pH } 5.20 \pm 0.05$).
   * Add $250.0\text{ g}$ Tanzanian dal. Maintain in water bath at $55^\circ\text{C} \pm 1^\circ\text{C}$ for **20 minutes** (with 40 kHz ultrasonic bath) or **40 minutes** (static soak).
3. **For POT 5 (Steam Flaking Prototype):**
   * Place $250.0\text{ g}$ dry Tanzanian dal in a single layer on a perforated steaming rack or idli steamer over boiling water.
   * Cover with lid. Steam for exactly **5 minutes**.
   * Immediately remove hot dal ($80^\circ\text{C} - 85^\circ\text{C}$) onto a clean cutting board.
   * Take a standard kitchen rolling pin. Apply firm, moderate downwards pressure and roll once across the warm grains.
   * *Target:* Grain must remain intact as whole split cotyledons, but with microscopic hairline surface cracks (no crumbling into powder).

---

### Phase 2: Dewatering, Rinsing & Surface pH Verification
1. Pour steeped dal from POT 3 into the stainless steel sieve. Allow free liquor to drain into a catch basin for 60 seconds.
2. Hold the sieve under a gentle stream or spray of clean potable water ($250\text{ mL}$ total rinse volume, ~15 seconds).
3. Shake sieve to remove entrained surface droplets.
4. **Quality Check (Surface pH):**
   * Press a wet pH strip directly against the surface of 5 dal cotyledons.
   * For POT 3, the surface pH must read **$\le 7.0$**. (If $>7.5$, perform a second 50 mL water rinse).
   * For POT 4, the surface pH must read **$5.2 - 5.6$**.
5. Repeat identical sieve drainage and rinse for POT 4.

---

### Phase 3: Standardized Pressure Cooking Protocol

Conduct cooking tests in the standard 3-Liter Hawkins Classic pressure cooker:

#### Cooking Recipe Parameters:
* **Dal Quantity per Cooking Run:** $100.0\text{ g}$ (dry equivalent basis).
  * For POT 1 & POT 6 (dry unsoaked): Add exactly $100.0\text{ g}$ dal $+ 300\text{ mL}$ water ($1:3.0$ ratio).
  * For POT 2, 3, 4 (wet pre-soaked dal): Weigh wet drained dal. (A $100\text{ g}$ dry equivalent has absorbed $\sim 28\text{ g}$ water, so wet weight will be $\sim 128\text{ g}$). Add $272\text{ mL}$ fresh water (maintaining exact total $300\text{ mL}$ water: $100\text{ g}$ dry dal ratio).
  * Add **$1.0\text{ g}$ salt ($\text{NaCl}$)** and **$0.25\text{ g}$ turmeric powder** (standard domestic baseline).

#### Burner & Valve Operation:
1. Lock pressure cooker lid. Place deadweight valve on the vent tube.
2. Place cooker on burner. Set heat to **HIGH (1,200 W / full gas flame)**. Start stopwatch at $t = 0.00$.
3. Observe venting. When internal gauge pressure reaches $103.9\text{ kPa}$, the deadweight valve lifts with a loud steam discharge (**WHISTLE 1**).
   * Record exact time $t_{\text{whistle 1}}$ (typically $5.5 - 6.5\text{ minutes}$).
4. Immediately reduce burner to **MEDIUM (600 W / medium gas flame)**.
5. Wait for the second steam discharge (**WHISTLE 2**).
   * Record exact time $t_{\text{whistle 2}}$ (typically $9.0 - 11.0\text{ minutes}$).
6. **For 2-Whistle Test Pots (POT 3, POT 4, POT 5):**
   * Turn off burner **IMMEDIATELY after Whistle 2 completes**.
   * Leave cooker untouched on the bench for **natural pressure release** ($7 - 8\text{ minutes}$).
   * Do NOT lift the weight manually with a fork. When safety valve drops, unlock lid.
7. **For Control Pots (POT 1, POT 2, POT 6):**
   * If dal is not tender after 2 whistles, continue counting whistles until full tenderness is reached (POT 1 typically requires 6 whistles; POT 6 Indian dal requires 3 whistles).

## 5. Dual-Gate Tenderness & Core Inspection (No False Passes)

Immediately upon opening cooker, perform the dual-gate physical assessment:

### Gate 1: Bulk Cotyledon Softness (Back-of-Spoon Test)
1. Scoop 10 cooked cotyledons onto a flat white plate.
2. Press gently with the curved back of a standard stainless steel tablespoon.
3. **Scoring:**
   * **Score 3 (Tender / Mashable):** Grain yields under light finger pressure, mashing smoothly into dal purée without resisting. $\to$ **PASS**
   * **Score 2 (Firm / Rubbery):** Grain flattens but feels elastic and rubbery. $\to$ **MARGINAL**
   * **Score 1 (Hard / Uncooked):** Grain resists pressure; requires hard force to crack. $\to$ **FAIL**

### Gate 2: Geometric Core Bisection Test (Anti-Raw-Center Verification)
*Prevents the classic failure mode where soft gelatinized outer shells hide a hard, chalky raw center (Ref: FAIL-0010).*
1. Select 5 representative cooked cotyledons.
2. Place on cutting board. Bisect each cotyledon exactly in half transversely using a fresh razor blade.
3. Inspect the cross-section under good lighting:
   * **100% Homogeneous (PASS):** Entire cross-section from outer surface to inner geometric center is uniform translucent yellow with no opaque white spots.
   * **Chalky Core Defect (FAIL):** A distinct opaque white circular disk or dry starch ring remains visible at the geometric center.

## 6. Organoleptic Taste & Aftertaste Evaluation

Conduct a blind tasting panel with 3 independent evaluators using standard Indian culinary preparations:

### Evaluation Metrics (9-Point Hedonic Scale):
* **9:** Excellent, superlative authentic dal flavor, rich creamy texture.
* **8:** Very good, traditional home-cooked dal quality, pleasant nutty finish.
* **7:** Good, acceptable commercial dal, clean finish.
* **5:** Neither like nor dislike; bland or slightly dry.
* **3:** Dislike moderately; noticeable chemical, bitter, or soapy note.
* **1:** Completely unacceptable; intense caustic or chemical burn.

### Specific Flavor Defects Checklist (Check Box if Detected):
- [ ] **Alkaline / Soapy Note:** Caustic, slippery mouthfeel on tongue (indicates unrinsed $\text{NaHCO}_3$).
- [ ] **Astringent / Puckering Note:** Dryness on sides of tongue (indicates polyphosphate or polyphenol leaching).
- [ ] **Sour / Acidic Note:** Tart, sharp citric taste (indicates $\text{pH} < 4.8$ or unbuffered citric acid).
- [ ] **Bitter Note:** Lingering throat bitterness (indicates high potassium salts $>1500\text{ mg}$ or bitter peptides).
- [ ] **Grassy / Beany Off-Flavor:** Cardboard/raw green bean aroma (indicates uninhibited lipoxygenase hexanal).

### Culinary Matrix Pairing Test:
Prepare three small bowls with the cooked dal from POT 3, POT 4, and POT 5:
1. **Plain Dal:** Cooked dal $+ 0.5\%\text{ NaCl} + 0.1\%\text{ turmeric}$. (Detects subtle intrinsic aftertastes).
2. **Dal Tadka:** Tempered in ghee with cumin seeds, mustard seeds, green chili, and hing. (Evaluates Maillard aroma harmony).
3. **Sambar:** Simmered with tamarind pulp, sambar powder, and drumsticks/carrots. (Acidic tamarind completely neutralizes any trace bicarbonate into $\text{CO}_2$ gas).
