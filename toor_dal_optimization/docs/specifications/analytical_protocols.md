# Analytical Testing Protocols: Phase 1 Toor Dal Processing Optimization

This document outlines the standard operating procedures (SOPs) and methodologies for the Phase 1 Comparative Analytical Study between Tanzanian and Indian Toor Dal samples.

## 1. Proximate & Fundamental Analysis

### 1.1 Moisture Content
* **Method:** AOAC 925.10 (Oven drying method)
* **Protocol:** 5g of ground sample dried in an air oven at 105 deg C until constant weight is achieved.
* **Objective:** Establish baseline hydration levels and calculate dry matter basis.

### 1.2 Crude Protein
* **Method:** Kjeldahl Method (AOAC 920.87)
* **Protocol:** Digestion in concentrated H2SO4, followed by distillation and titration. Nitrogen to protein conversion factor (N x 6.25).
* **Objective:** Determine if protein matrix density contributes to hardness.

### 1.3 Total Starch Content
* **Method:** Megazyme Total Starch Assay Kit (AA/AMG) (AOAC 996.11)
* **Protocol:** Enzymatic hydrolysis of starch to D-glucose, followed by colorimetric measurement at 510 nm.

## 2. Starch Characterization

### 2.1 Amylose / Amylopectin Ratio
* **Method:** Megazyme Amylose/Amylopectin Assay Kit
* **Protocol:** Precipitation of amylopectin using Concanavalin A (Con A).
* **Objective:** Higher amylose typically correlates with higher retrogradation and harder cooking textures.

### 2.2 Gelatinization Thermodynamics (DSC)
* **Method:** Differential Scanning Calorimetry (DSC)
* **Protocol:** 3 mg of dal flour mixed with 6 uL of distilled water, sealed in aluminum pans. Heated from 20 deg C to 120 deg C at 10 deg C/min.
* **Outputs:** Onset temperature (To), peak temperature (Tp), conclusion temperature (Tc), and enthalpy of gelatinization (Delta-H).

## 3. Cell Wall & Cooking-Resistance Drivers

### 3.1 Pectin Analysis & Cation Complexing
* **Method:** Fractionation of pectins (Water-soluble, Chelator-soluble, Sodium Carbonate-soluble).
* **Cation Assay (Ca2+, Mg2+):** Atomic Absorption Spectroscopy (AAS) following acid digestion (HNO3/HClO4).
* **Objective:** To determine if the Tanzanian Dal possesses a higher degree of cross-linked pectates in the middle lamella, a primary driver of the Hard-to-Cook (HTC) defect.

### 3.2 Phytic Acid (Phytate) Content
* **Method:** Colorimetric Wade Reagent Assay
* **Objective:** Phytic acid chelates Ca2+ and Mg2+. If phytates are broken down (e.g., via prolonged storage/phytase activity), these ions migrate to the cell wall to harden pectins. Lower phytic acid often correlates with HTC defect.

### 3.3 Instrumental Hardness (Texture Analyzer)
* **Method:** Texture Profile Analysis (TPA) using TA.XTPlus.
* **Protocol:** Compression test of soaked and cooked cotyledons to 50% strain using a cylindrical probe. 
* **Metrics:** Hardness (N), cohesiveness, springiness.

## 4. Hydration Kinetics & Water Absorption Index (WAI)

### 4.1 Isothermal Hydration (Peleg's Model)
* **Method:** Gravimetric water uptake.
* **Protocol:** 10g of whole dal soaked in 100 mL of distilled water at controlled temperatures (25 deg C, 50 deg C, 80 deg C). Weights measured at 15, 30, 60, 120, 240, and 480 minutes.
* **Modeling:** Data fitted to Peleg's equation: M(t) = M0 + t / (K1 + K2 * t)
  * K1: Peleg rate constant (mass transfer, min/%).
  * K2: Peleg capacity constant (maximum absorption, %^-1).

### 4.2 Water Absorption Index (WAI) & Water Solubility Index (WSI)
* **Protocol:** Anderson et al. (1969) method involving centrifugation of aqueous dal dispersions.
