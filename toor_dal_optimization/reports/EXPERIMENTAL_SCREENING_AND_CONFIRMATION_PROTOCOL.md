## 1. Objective & Scientific Method

The primary objective of this experimental protocol is to identify and validate the lowest-cost, technically feasible treatment that reduces the domestic cooking time of imported Tanzanian split/dehulled pigeon pea (*Cajanus cajan*) to match the Indian dal benchmark ($\approx 3\text{ whistles / } \le 15\text{ minutes}$), while preserving natural cotyledon morphology, taste, color, solids recovery ($\ge 95.5\%$), and FSSAI compliance.

### Non-Negotiable Experimental Governance
1. **Replicate Independence:** Individual beans drawn from one pot or tub are subsamples (pseudoreplicates), not process replicates. An independent replicate ($n$) requires an independently prepared soaking batch, independent drying cycle, and separate pressure-cooker run.
2. **Post-Drying Product Requirement:** Treatments must be evaluated on the **final re-dried and cooled product** ($10.0 \pm 0.5\%$ moisture, $a_w \le 0.65$), not on wet soaked grains. A treatment that softens dal while wet but loses its advantage upon drying fails the commercial product specification.
3. **Primary Endpoint:** Standardized time (minutes) under pressure ($115^\circ\text{C}, 15\text{ psi}$) to reach an objective physical tenderness threshold ($< 45\text{ kPa}$ peak compression force / complete extrusion through $1.0\text{ mm}$ sieve). Whistle count is recorded as secondary operational context.

- **Flaked Dal Route:** Excluded from primary screening. Flaking permanently flattens dal into breakfast-style flakes and increases rancidity. Flaking is a separate product concept requiring marketing approval.

## 4. Standardized Cooking & Physical Tenderness Protocol

To eliminate operator bias and cooker geometry confounding, all cooking tests must adhere to this standardized procedure:

```
[ STANDARDIZED DOMESTIC PRESSURE COOKER RIG ]
  - Cooker: Standard 3.0 Litre Stainless Steel Pressure Cooker (Hawkins / Prestige).
  - Operating Pressure: 15.0 psi (1.03 bar gauge), Saturated Steam Temp = 115.0°C.
  - Heat Source: 1500 W Electric Hot Plate / Induction Unit with calibrated power control.
  - Cooker Charge: 100.0 g raw/treated dal + 350.0 g standardized potable water (1:3.5 mass ratio).
  - Temperature Logging: Type-K Thermocouple inserted via pressure-sealed gland to cotyledon bed.
```

### 4.1 Step-by-Step Cooking Execution
1. **Weighing & Pre-Wetting:** Weigh exactly $100.0 \pm 0.1\text{ g}$ of dal into the pressure cooker. Add $350.0 \pm 1.0\text{ g}$ of water at $25.0^\circ\text{C}$.
2. **Sealing & Heat Ramp:** Seal the cooker lid with a new silicone gasket. Place on the heating element at $1200\text{ W}$ heat input. Start the digital timer ($t = 0.00$).
3. **Pressure Onset & Whistle Logging:**
   - Record timestamp when steam vent begins steady discharge.
   - Place standard $15\text{ psi}$ deadweight regulator onto vent pipe.
   - Record exact timestamp and temperature of **every whistle vent cycle** ($W_1, W_2, W_3, \dots$).
4. **Depressurization & Quenching:**
   - At predetermined whistle counts (or pre-registered test times), remove cooker from heat.
   - Allow natural pressure drop for $2\text{ minutes}$, followed by cold-water lid quench to atmospheric pressure ($0\text{ psi}$).
   - Immediately drain cooked dal over a $1.0\text{ mm}$ stainless steel sieve.
5. **Physical Tenderness Measurement:**
   - **Tactile Squeeze Test (Narasimha & Desikachar standard):** Press 20 individual grains between thumb and forefinger or between two parallel acrylic plates under $2.0\text{ N}$ force. Grains are scored as:
     - *Hard Core (0):* Firm central unhydrated core detectable.
     - *Partially Cooked (1):* Yields under pressure but gritty center.
     - *Uniformly Soft (2):* Smooth, creamy cotyledon paste without gritty core.
   - **Instrumental Firmness (where Texture Analyzer is available):** Single-grain compression on TA.XT Plus with $35\text{ mm}$ flat cylinder probe. Record peak force ($\text{N}$ and $\text{kPa}$). Fully cooked endpoint is defined as peak stress $< 45.0\text{ kPa}$.
   - **Solids Dispersed (%):** Collect cooking water, evaporate aliquot at $105^\circ\text{C}$, and calculate percentage of dal solids dispersed into cooking broth.

## 5. Statistical Power, Sample Size, and Decision Boundaries

To avoid making unsubstantiated "5-sigma" claims, sample sizes and hypothesis tests are derived from classical statistical power principles:

### 5.1 Formal Hypothesis Formulation
- **Null Hypothesis ($H_0$):** $\mu_{\text{treated}} \ge \mu_{\text{control}} - 4.0\text{ minutes}$ (Treatment fails to achieve at least a $4.0\text{ min}$ reduction in pressure-cooking time).
- **Alternative Hypothesis ($H_1$):** $\mu_{\text{treated}} < \mu_{\text{control}} - 4.0\text{ minutes}$ (Treatment meaningfully reduces cooking time by $> 4.0\text{ minutes}$, moving from ~6 whistles to $\le 3.5$ whistles).

### 5.2 Two-Sample Welch's t-Test Power Boundaries
For an experimental design with $n_1 = 4$ (Untreated Control) and $n_2 = 4$ (Treated Candidate CAND-004), degrees of freedom $df = 6$:
- Expected mean cooking time difference: $\Delta\mu = 22.0 - 13.8 = 8.2\text{ minutes}$ ($492\text{ seconds}$).
- Critical $t$-value for statistical significance at $\alpha = 0.005$ (two-tailed): $t_{\text{crit}} = 3.707$.
- Required $t$-statistic for a $5\sigma$ standard ($\alpha = 2.87 \times 10^{-7}$): $t_{5\sigma} = 22.46$.

$$t_{\text{obs}} = \frac{492}{s_{\text{pooled}} \sqrt{\frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{4}}} = \frac{695.8}{s_{\text{pooled}}}$$

| Kitchen Standard Deviation ($s_{\text{pooled}}$) | $t_{\text{obs}}$ ($n=4$) | Exact $p$-Value ($df=6$) | Empirical Significance Level | Stage 1 Decision Gate Status |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **$s = 15\text{ seconds}$** ($0.25\text{ min}$) | $46.39$ | $1.2 \times 10^{-8}$ | **$> 5.5\sigma$** | **PASSES $5\sigma$ REQUIREMENT** |
| **$s = 30\text{ seconds}$** ($0.50\text{ min}$) | $23.19$ | $2.4 \times 10^{-7}$ | **$\approx 5.0\sigma$** | **PASSES $5\sigma$ REQUIREMENT** |
| **$s = 60\text{ seconds}$** ($1.00\text{ min}$) | $11.60$ | $1.4 \times 10^{-5}$ | **$4.22\sigma$** | **PASSES $\alpha=0.005$; FAILS $5\sigma$** |
| **$s = 90\text{ seconds}$** ($1.50\text{ min}$) | $7.73$ | $2.4 \times 10^{-4}$ | **$3.67\sigma$** | **PASSES $\alpha=0.005$; FAILS $5\sigma$** |
| **$s = 120\text{ seconds}$** ($2.00\text{ min}$) | $5.80$ | $1.1 \times 10^{-3}$ | **$3.25\sigma$** | **PASSES $\alpha=0.005$; FAILS $5\sigma$** |

### 5.3 Staged Stopping Rules
1. **Pilot Screening (Phase B):** Run $n = 2$ batches per candidate. If a candidate fails to achieve $< 17.0\text{ minutes}$ ($< 4.5\text{ whistles}$), or if sensory shows severe soapy defect ($< 6.0$), immediately terminate that candidate.
2. **Confirmatory Replication (Phase C):** Expand the top 2 candidates to $n = 4$ independent process replicates.
3. **Acceptance Threshold:** Stage 1 officially passes and unlocks Stage 2 only if:
   - $t_{\text{obs}} \ge 3.707$ ($p \le 0.005$) against control.
   - Mean cooking time $\le 15.0\text{ minutes}$ ($\le 3.5\text{ whistles}$).
   - Whole-grain solids recovery $\ge 95.5\%$.
   - Retained sodium $< 350\text{ mg / 100g}$.
   - Blinded sensory score $\ge 7.5 / 9.0$.

## 6. Blinded Sensory Acceptance Protocol

To ensure chemical softening does not introduce off-flavors, yellowing, or texture defects, a sensory panel evaluates cooked samples:
- **Panel:** 8 semi-trained panellists familiar with traditional Indian Toor Dal preparation.
- **Serving:** Dal cooked with $0.5\%\ \text{NaCl}$ and $0.1\%\ \text{turmeric}$ (standard tadka preparation) served warm ($50^\circ\text{C}$) in coded, randomized white bowls.
- **Scale:** 9-point Hedonic Scale (1 = Dislike Extremely, 5 = Neither Like nor Dislike, 9 = Like Extremely).
- **Attributes Scored:**
  1. *Appearance & Color:* Natural bright golden yellow without gray or brownish tinge.
  2. *Cotyledon Texture:* Creamy, smooth mouthfeel without gritty unhydrated cores or slimy skin.
  3. *Flavor & Odor:* Clean, nutty pulse aroma free of soapy, alkaline, or chemical notes.
  4. *Overall Acceptability:* Must score $\ge 7.5 / 9.0$ for commercial release.
