**Project:** Harakutir Toor Dal Diagnostic R&D (Phase 1)  
**Standard:** Strict Evidence Hierarchy (SOW §2.2 & §16–17)  
**Classification Rules:**  
- `CONFIRMED_DIFFERENCE`: Traceable measured divergence between samples.
- `STRONGLY_ASSOCIATED`: Statistically or biochemically linked to cooking duration.
- `POTENTIAL_MECHANISM`: Supported by pulse science; subject to Phase 2 validation.
- `UNLIKELY_EXCLUDED`: Demonstrated to be non-causal or statistically insignificant.

## 2. SCIENTIFIC EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS

### The Excluded Factors:
1. **Seed-Coat Resistance:**
   Earlier hypotheses suggested that Tanzanian dal has a tougher or thicker hull that blocks moisture. Peer-reviewed research on 6 pigeon pea genotypes (*Machinjili et al. 2025*) disproved this: seed-coat thickness showed **no statistically significant relationship** with cooking duration. Furthermore, commercial Toor Dal is **dehulled and split**—the seed coat is physically absent during domestic cooking.
2. **Moisture Diffusion Blockade:**
   Vectorized spherical diffusion modeling confirms that at 100°C, hydration reaches equilibrium in 15–20 minutes. Moisture is physically present in the cotyledons, yet softening does not occur until 55 minutes.

### The True Root Cause:
The delay is strictly a **chemical dissolution problem** in the middle lamella. Divalent calcium bridges prevent cells from separating under heat. Until these ionic bridges are cleaved (either through prolonged boiling or monovalent ion exchange), the grain remains perceived as raw and hard-cored.
