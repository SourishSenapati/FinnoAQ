# MINIMUM LABORATORY TEST PLAN: PRUNED EVIDENCE STRATEGY

**Project:** FinnoaQ Toor Dal Diagnostic R&D (Phase 1)  
**Objective:** Minimize external laboratory expenditure while obtaining decisive, defensible scientific proof of the root cause.  
**Governing Principle:** Screen selectively; do not perform outsourced assays on parameters that can be verified in-house or resolved through literature.  

---

## 1. THREE-TIERED TESTING ARCHITECTURE

To avoid spending the full ₹1.73 lakh SOW budget blindly, the testing architecture is structured into three discrete stages:

1. **Tier 1 (In-House Zero-Cost Screening):** Performed with basic kitchen tools and grocery-grade reagents (< ₹100).
2. **Tier 2 (Targeted Accredited Verification):** A single minimal laboratory assay if an official NABL certificate is legally required (< ₹5,000).
3. **Tier 3 (Conditional Full Scope):** Triggered ONLY if Tier 1 and Tier 2 fail to discriminate the mechanism.

---

## 2. TIER 1: IN-HOUSE ZERO-COST DIAGNOSTIC SCREEN (PACKAGE A)

### Assay A1: Physical Baseline & Defect Ratio (Cost: ₹0)
- **Equipment:** Digital precision scale (0.1g) + digital vernier caliper (0.01mm).
- **Protocol:** Measure 1000-grain weight, mean cotyledon diameter ($2R$), thickness, and percentage of broken/split cotyledons.
- **Decision Value:** Establishes geometric baseline to normalize diffusion equations.

### Assay A2: Controlled Gravimetric Hydration Curve (Cost: ₹0)
- **Protocol:** Soak 50.0g of Tanzanian dal and 50.0g of Indian dal in distilled/filtered water at room temperature (25°C).
- **Time Points:** Weigh drained grains at $t = 0, 15, 30, 60, 120, 240, 480\text{ min}$.
- **Decision Value:**
  - If Tanzanian dal absorbs water at the same rate as Indian dal $\to$ **Water uptake is NOT the problem**. Rule out porosity defects.
  - If Tanzanian dal shows an asymptotic plateau below 80% dry basis $\to$ Hydration barrier exists.

### Assay A3: The ₹50 Chemical Ion-Exchange Discrimination Assay (The Silver Bullet)
- **Hypothesis:** Does middle-lamella calcium pectate crosslinking cause the cooking delay?
- **Protocol:** Prepare four identical 50.0g batches:
  1. *Batch A (Negative Control):* Tanzanian dal in plain water.
  2. *Batch B (Monovalent Salt):* Tanzanian dal in 1.0% Food-grade Table Salt ($\text{NaCl}$) solution.
  3. *Batch C (Alkaline Chelator):* Tanzanian dal in 0.75% Food-grade Baking Soda ($\text{NaHCO}_3$) solution.
  4. *Batch D (Benchmark Control):* Indian domestic dal in plain water.
- **Cooking & Endpoint:** Soak for 20 minutes, then cook in identical boiling water. Check softening every 5 minutes using the **glass-slide compression test** (a grain pressed between two glass slides with a 500g weight flattens completely without a hard chalky core).
- **Decision Rules:**
  - **If Batch C (baking soda) cooks in 20–25 minutes (matching Indian Batch D):**
    $\implies$ **Root cause is 100% verified as divalent cation-pectate crosslinking.**
    $\implies$ **Commercial solution is simultaneously discovered.**
    $\implies$ **Save 100% of remaining laboratory funds.**

## 3. TIER 2: MINIMAL OUTSOURCED ACCREDITED TEST (IF CERTIFICATE REQUIRED)

If Technical Advisory Panel or an investor requires an accredited NABL laboratory certificate, order **ONLY ONE SINGLE COMBINED TEST**:

- **Assay:** Basic Oven Moisture Content (AOAC 925.10) + ICP-OES Minerals ($\text{Ca}, \text{Mg}, \text{K}, \text{Na}$).
- **Samples:** 1 Tanzanian lot vs 1 Indian lot (2 samples total).
- **Laboratories in Kolkata:** Eureka Analytical Services Pvt. Ltd. or Mitra S.K. Pvt. Ltd.
- **Estimated Market Fee:** ₹3,500 – ₹4,500 + GST.
- **Decision Value:** Mathematically confirms whether the divalent-to-monovalent ratio $[\text{Ca} + \text{Mg}] / [\text{K} + \text{Na}]$ is elevated in the Tanzanian lot.
