## 1. Executive Summary: Evidence & Literature Foundation

The hard-to-cook (HTC) defect in Tanzanian pigeon pea (*Cajanus cajan*) is a physical and biochemical phenomenon rooted in the middle lamella pectin matrix. This register reviews the peer-reviewed scientific literature and Indian food safety statutory regulations (FSSAI) to establish evidence-based parameter bounds, screen treatment mechanisms, and determine product-category compliance.

### Critical Product-Format Distinctions
- **Intact Split/Dehulled Dal (FinnoaQ Target):** Decorticated, split cotyledons that retain their natural lenticular form, texture, appearance, and cooking behavior in home pressure cookers.
- **Whole Pigeon Pea (with Seed Coat):** Intact seeds with fibrous testa. The seed coat acts as a severe barrier to water, ion diffusion, and thermal transport. Literature on whole pigeon pea cannot be directly applied to split dal without adjusting for the absence of the seed coat.
- **Flaked Dal / Pre-Cooked Convenience Products:** Mechanically rolled, flattened flakes or partially gelatinized grains. Flaking permanently alters the consumer product form into a specialty convenience item and undergoes accelerated lipid oxidation during ambient storage.

## 3. Indian Food Safety & Regulatory Register (FSSAI)

An engineering process is commercially invalid if the resulting product cannot be legally sold as Toor Dal in India. This section audits Indian food regulations regarding food additives, processing aids, product classification, and mandatory labeling.

### 3.1 Primary Statutory References
1. **Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006** (Act No. 34 of 2006).
2. **Food Safety and Standards (Food Products Standards and Food Additives) Regulations, 2011** (FSS/FPS&FA), as amended through 2024.
3. **Food Safety and Standards (Labelling and Display) Regulations, 2020** (FSS/L&D).
4. **Food Safety and Standards (Processing Aids) Regulations, 2018 / 2021 Amendments**.

## 4. Synthesis of Findings & Guardrails

1. **Flaking Route Disqualified from Core Scope:** Sethi et al. (2014) proved that flaking achieves $6\text{ min}$ cooking time, but causes product deformation, storage instability (3-fold cooking time increase after 10 months), and lipid auto-oxidation. Flaking is classified as a distinct specialty convenience product, requiring explicit client marketing sign-off.
2. **Ultrasound Disqualified for Split Dal:** Vásquez et al. (2021) demonstrated ultrasound effectiveness only because it eroded the tough outer seed coat of whole pigeon peas. For dehulled split dal, monovalent diffusion into open cotyledon pores occurs rapidly without sonication; purchasing high-power ultrasound transducers is uneconomical.
3. **Additive Basis Confirmed:** Sodium bicarbonate at $0.50\%\ \text{w/w}$ is legally defensible under FSSAI Table 3 (GMP) provided it is properly declared on the ingredient panel. Retained elemental sodium ($53.9\text{ mg/100g}$) is completely compliant.
4. **Mandatory Post-Drying Testing:** Literature universally confirms that pulse cooking time increases during storage. The experimental protocol must test the re-dried, conditioned product ($10.0\%$ moisture) after storage, not merely wet soaked grains.
