# Honey Creme (Creamed Honey) Industrial Production: SOP

## 1. Process Fundamentals and Microrheology
Honey creme (creamed honey) is a controlled micro-crystallization of natural honey using the Dyce process. The goal is to induce rapid nucleation to create millions of sub-30 micron crystals (mostly Glucose Monohydrate) to form a stable, spreadable matrix.

### 1.1 Target Specifications
- **Crystal Size Distribution:** D50 < 25.0 microns
- **Texture Profile:** Butter-smooth (Spreadability index 7.5)
- **Phase Stability:** No liquid separation (12 months @ 25°C)
- **Hardness (Penetrometer):** 1.5 mm - 2.5 mm
- **Moisture Content:** 17.5% - 18.5% (Critical for stability)

## 3. Detailed Process Blueprint

### Phase 1: Clarification and Pasteurization (T-Minus 0-4 hrs)
1. **Liquification:** Heat raw honey in 1,000L kettle to 65°C for 30 minutes. This dissolves all existing "wild" crystals.
2. **Filtration:** Pass the hot honey through a 100-mesh duplex filter.
3. **De-aeration:** Apply 0.8 bar vacuum for 60 minutes. Air bubbles catalyze un-intended large-crystal growth.
4. **Cooling:** Flash-cool using a plate heat exchanger (PHE) down to 25°C.

### Phase 2: Seeding and Inoculation (T-Minus 4-8 hrs)
1. **Seed Preparation:** Warm the "seed" honey (pre-creamed) to 25°C to match the base honey temperature.
2. **Inoculation:** Gradually blend 10% seed into the 90% base using a low-shear paddle mixer (15-20 RPM).
3. **Homogenization:** Mix for 60 minutes to ensure every 1ml of honey contains thousands of micro-crystal "nuclei".
4. **Consistency check:** Final mix should be opaque and have a matte sheen instead of transparent.

### Phase 3: Primary Crystallization - The "Dyce" Cycle (Days 1 - 4)
1. **Packaging:** Fill the inoculated honey into final retail containers or 50kg bulk pails.
2. **Thermal Control:** Place containers in a temperature-controlled crystallization room ("The Dyce Room").
3. **Target Temp:** 13.5°C - 14.5°C. This is the optimal thermodynamic window for Glucose Monohydrate growth.
4. **Crystallization Speed:** CUDA models confirm 90% set as achieved in 72 hours under steady 14°C conditions.
5. **Monitoring:** Probe sample batches for hardness and crystal size D50 every 24 hours.

### Phase 4: Creaming and Secondary Shearing (Optional - Days 4-5)
1. **Shearing:** If a softer, "whipped" texture is required, pass the set honey through a Scraped Surface Crystallizer (SSC).
2. **Aeration (Optional):** Infuse nitrogen at 5-10% by volume to create a light, aeration creme (Honey Mousse).
3. **Pumping:** Use progressive cavity pumps (Moyno type) to move the thick creme without breaking the crystal network.

### Phase 5: Maturation and Storage (Final Ops)
1. **Conditioning:** Store at 18-20°C for 24-48 hours before shipping.
2. **Texture Check:** Verify that the product does not "drip" when inverted.
3. **Palletizing:** Use refrigerated transport (set @ 18°C) for initial shipment to prevent de-crystallization in summer.

## 4. Machinery & CAPEX Requirements
- **Heating/Cooling:** Jacketed SS316 Kettles + Plate Heat Exchanger (PHE).
- **Crystallization Room:** Cold Store with ± 0.5°C tolerance.
- **Mixer:** SS316 Paddle Agitator (Variable Speed).
- **SSC:** Scraped Surface Heat Exchanger (Waukesha or similar).

## 5. Potential Failure Modes and Mitigations (FMEA)
| Failure Mode | Root Cause | Mitigation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Grittiness / Sandiness** | Coarse Crystals (> 50 um) | Inadequate Pasteurization or Poor Seed Dispersion. |
| **Layer Separation** | High Moisture (> 20%) | Ensure honey harvest brix > 81.5% or concentrate pre-seeding. |
| **Layer Separation** | Temp Fluctuation (> 18°C) | Strict cold-chain management during the Dyce cycle. |
